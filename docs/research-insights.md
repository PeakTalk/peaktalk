# Anonymized research insights

## Evidence boundary

These notes are derived from a small, non-representative set of exploratory
conversations. They contain no participant names, contact details, direct
quotes, raw transcripts, employer details, or meeting-specific material.

The findings are product hypotheses, not statistical evidence of demand or a
claim that the target market has been validated.

## Derived needs and product implications

| Derived need | Product implication | Current interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Preparation starts from a position that must survive a real decision, not from a generic topic. | Ask for the target decision, audience, constraints, and working material. | Keep the primary object a concrete meeting case. |
| Users need to see where their argument can be attacked before rehearsing it. | Separate weak-point discovery from the adversarial question loop. | Questions should trace back to evidence gaps or explicit assumptions. |
| A realistic challenge is more useful than encouragement without context. | Use stakeholder-specific objections and require concrete answers. | Do not frame the product as confidence coaching. |
| The result must remain usable after the interactive session. | Produce a concise Defense Brief with arguments, risks, evidence gaps, and next steps. | Optimize for review immediately before the meeting. |
| Long-form voice interaction creates avoidable friction and ambiguity. | Keep the simulation text-first; optional voice input only composes text. | Microphone access must never gate completion. |
| Work material may be sensitive even when it contains no formal secret. | Make data handling, scope, and model limitations visible at input time. | Encourage redaction and avoid collecting material that is unnecessary for the case. |

## What remains unproven

- whether users return for multiple different high-stakes meetings;
- which meeting types create repeatable value rather than one-off curiosity;
- how often the Defense Brief changes a real preparation decision;
- the minimum material needed for a useful pressure scan;
- which privacy controls are required before users submit sensitive workplace
  documents;
- willingness to pay outside a test-stage product flow.

## Next validation work

Future discovery should use explicit consent, collect the minimum necessary
data, and separate research records from the source repository. Useful measures
would focus on completed preparation cases, repeat use, corrections made to the
underlying argument, and whether the brief was used before a real conversation.
None should be presented as a success metric until a measurement method and
sample boundary are documented.
